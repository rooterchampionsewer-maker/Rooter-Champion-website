What you might notice

  • Dirty water or sewage coming up in a tub, shower or floor drain
  • Sewage flowing out of the outdoor cleanout cap
  • Toilets that will not flush and are filling with dirty water
  • A strong sewage smell along with rising water

Likely causes

  • A fully blocked main sewer line, often from roots, wipes or grease
  • A broken or collapsed section of sewer pipe
  • Less often, a blockage in the city sewer main. If neighbors have the same problem, call your city's public works line too.

Safe things to try first

  1. Stop using all water: no flushing, showers, laundry, dishwasher or running faucets.
  2. Keep children and pets away from the area.
  3. Avoid touching sewage. If you must, wear rubber gloves and boots, and wash well afterward.
  4. Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances that are near standing water.
  5. Open windows for fresh air if you can do it safely.
  6. Take photos for your records and your insurance.
Why we don’t recommend chemical drain cleaners: Skip the chemical drain cleaners. They are strong caustics or acids that can burn skin and eyes, can damage older pipes and fittings, and often only eat a small hole through the clog. When they do not work, the drain is left full of chemical water that can splash you or whoever opens the line next. Never mix drain products, because some combinations give off toxic fumes.

If water starts rising or you see sewage, stop and call. Don’t take apart anything you’re not comfortable putting back together.

Common questions

Should I book online for a sewage backup?

No. Call us at 714-305-9263 so we can talk you through it and get you on the schedule as quickly as we can.

Can I pour a chemical drain cleaner into the backed-up drain?

No. It will not clear a main line blockage, and it turns the standing water into a chemical hazard for you and for the technician who has to open the line.

How do I know if it is the city's problem or mine?

If your neighbors are backing up too, the city sewer main may be blocked, so call your city's public works line. If it is only your home, the blockage is usually in your own sewer line.
