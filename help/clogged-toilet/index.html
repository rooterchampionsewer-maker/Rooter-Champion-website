Clogged Toilet in La Habra

A clogged toilet is stressful, especially when the water is rising. Take a breath: most toilet clogs are close to the bowl and clear with the right plunger. Here is what to do, and what not to do.

What you might notice

  • The water level rises high in the bowl after flushing
  • The toilet drains slowly or only partly
  • Gurgling or bubbling in the bowl
  • Water comes up in the tub or shower when you flush (this points to the main line)

Likely causes

  • Too much toilet paper at once
  • Wipes, including ones labeled "flushable", paper towels or feminine products
  • A small object such as a toy, cap or toothbrush
  • A clog or tree roots in the main sewer line, if more than one drain is affected

Safe things to try first

  1. Do not flush again. If the bowl is high, take the lid off the tank and lift the flapper closed, or turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the water.
  2. Use a flange plunger (the kind with a soft extension at the bottom). Seat it fully over the hole and give 10 to 15 firm push-pull strokes.
  3. If the bowl is nearly empty, add some water so the plunger cup is covered.
  4. A toilet auger (closet auger) can reach a few feet past the bowl. Use one made for toilets so you do not scratch the porcelain.
  5. Put old towels around the base in case it overflows.
Why we don’t recommend chemical drain cleaners: Skip the chemical drain cleaners. They are strong caustics or acids that can burn skin and eyes, can damage older pipes and fittings, and often only eat a small hole through the clog. When they do not work, the drain is left full of chemical water that can splash you or whoever opens the line next. Never mix drain products, because some combinations give off toxic fumes.

If water starts rising or you see sewage, stop and call. Don’t take apart anything you’re not comfortable putting back together.

When to call a pro

  • The plunger and toilet auger did not clear it
  • Water shows up in the tub, shower or floor drain when you flush
  • Several toilets or drains are slow at the same time
  • The toilet clogs often even with normal use
  • Something hard, like a toy, went down and will not come back up

Common questions

Should I keep flushing to push the clog through?

No. Each flush adds more water to a bowl that cannot drain, and that is how toilets overflow. Stop flushing and plunge instead.

Are flushable wipes safe for my pipes?

We see wipes in a lot of clogged lines. Even wipes labeled "flushable" do not break down the way toilet paper does, so they can snag and build up in the pipe. Throw them in the trash.

Why does my bathtub fill up when I flush the toilet?

That usually means the main sewer line is partly blocked, so water from the toilet backs up into the lowest drain nearby. Stop using water and call us; this is not a toilet-only clog.
